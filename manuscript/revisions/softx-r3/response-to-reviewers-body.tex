\section*{Response to the Editor --- FHOPS
(SOFTX-D-26-00697R2)}\label{response-to-the-editor-fhops-softx-d-26-00697r2}

\textbf{Manuscript:} FHOPS: a reproducible optimisation and benchmarking
stack for forest harvest operations\\
\textbf{Decision:} Reconsideration following minor revision\\
\textbf{Editor:} Randall Sobie (Editor-in-Chief, SoftwareX)

We thank the editor for these helpful suggestions. All of them have been
adopted; the changes are limited to the abstract, one highlight, the
Table 1 caption, one item in Section 2, and one sentence of the
introduction, and are shown in the marked-up manuscript.

\subsubsection*{E.1 ``It is difficult to refer to other works in the
abstract, so it is better to be more general. (I am also uneasy with you
refering to the work of one of the authors in the
abstract)''}\label{e.1-it-is-difficult-to-refer-to-other-works-in-the-abstract-so-it-is-better-to-be-more-general.-i-am-also-uneasy-with-you-refering-to-the-work-of-one-of-the-authors-in-the-abstract}

\textbf{Response:} Agreed. The abstract no longer refers to the
systematic review or to any other work, including the authors' companion
studies: its last sentence now says only that design analyses and
deployment studies are left to future work. The review is cited only in
the introduction, where the self-citation is disclosed.

\subsubsection*{E.2 Suggested combined first
sentence}\label{e.2-suggested-combined-first-sentence}

\textbf{Response:} Adopted verbatim: ``Operational forest machine
scheduling remains dominated by site-specific tools that are difficult
to transfer, audit, and compare across studies, giving rise to calls for
reusable, transparent planning workflows.''

\subsubsection*{E.3 ``Our journal is multidisciplinary and the abstract
has words and acronyms that are unknown me (as a non-expert)
`SA/ILS/Tabu heuristics)'
?''}\label{e.3-our-journal-is-multidisciplinary-and-the-abstract-has-words-and-acronyms-that-are-unknown-me-as-a-non-expert-sailstabu-heuristics}

\textbf{Response:} The heuristics are now spelled out (``simulated
annealing, iterated local search, and tabu search heuristics''). We also
replaced other specialist wording in the abstract (``scenario contract''
is now ``scenario data format'', ``dataset ladder'' is now ``datasets of
increasing size'', and ``synthetic scaling tiers'' is now ``synthetic
instances for scaling tests''), and in the highlights ``MILP solvers''
is now ``exact solvers''. The acronyms SA, ILS and Tabu are now also
defined in the main text (Table 1 caption and Section 2).

\subsubsection*{E.4 ``You could drop `(tiny7, small21, med42)' as
well.''}\label{e.4-you-could-drop-tiny7-small21-med42-as-well.}

\textbf{Response:} Dropped, together with the abbreviation ``(BC)'',
which the abstract did not use again.

\subsubsection*{E.5 ``Main -20- you could drop the initials in the
bracket since you mention that it is from all 3
authors''}\label{e.5-main--20--you-could-drop-the-initials-in-the-bracket-since-you-mention-that-it-is-from-all-3-authors}

\textbf{Response:} Dropped. The sentence now reads ``Because all three
present authors are co-authors of that review, we disclose the
self-citation up front \ldots{}''.

\begin{abstract}
Operational forest machine scheduling remains dominated by site-specific tools that are difficult to transfer, audit, and compare across studies, giving rise to calls for reusable, transparent planning workflows. The Forest Harvesting Operations Planning System (FHOPS) addresses this need with an open-source operational planning platform built around a common scenario data format, a solver stack (mixed-integer programming plus simulated annealing, iterated local search, and tabu search heuristics), and a unified evaluation layer for schedule quality, robustness, and cost interpretation. We demonstrate the platform on reproducible British Columbia ground-based datasets of increasing size plus synthetic instances for scaling tests, with manuscript tables and figures generated directly from versioned benchmark assets. This paper documents the reusable software platform and baseline evidence; detailed rolling-horizon design analysis and broader deployment studies are treated as companion modelling work.
\end{abstract}
